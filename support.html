Our Mission

Modern societies depend on systems that were designed to operate independently. Reality no longer behaves that way.

Across energy, water, artificial intelligence, finance, ecosystems, infrastructure, and public institutions, our research identified the same recurring pattern: institutions consistently recognize systemic risk only after critical thresholds have already begun to close.

The consequence is not simply slower decision-making. It is the gradual, compounding loss of the opportunity to act while meaningful choices still exist. Our research suggests that one of the defining challenges of contemporary governance is the growing gap between the speed of systemic change and the capacity of institutions to respond. We define this structural disconnect as the Recognition-Action Gap.

Donations and institutional funding support three connected programs: the Amazon Living Lab, where field research links ecological systems, legal design, and community resilience; the Threshold Functions Protocol and Threshold Dynamics Research, which develop tools for identifying systemic risk before critical windows close; and the Fellowship Program, which prepares emerging researchers and practitioners to translate this work into durable public-interest institutions.
