01

Purpose

Clause 1.1 — Statement of Purpose

This Governance Manual (the "Manual") constitutes the master governance instrument of the Johann Christian Hasse Foundation (the "Foundation"), a Missouri nonprofit corporation recognized as a Section 501(c)(3) private foundation under the Internal Revenue Code. This Manual supplements the Foundation's Articles of Incorporation and Bylaws and provides the authoritative framework for Board governance, fiduciary oversight, and institutional conduct.

Clause 1.2 — Scope and Hierarchy

In the event of any conflict between this Manual and the Articles of Incorporation or Bylaws, the Articles of Incorporation and Bylaws shall control. In the event of any conflict between this Manual and any policy, resolution, or operational guideline, this Manual shall control unless the Board expressly provides otherwise by supermajority vote. All subordinate documents — including committee charters, policies, and protocols — shall be read consistently with this Manual.

Clause 1.3 — Guiding Principle

The Foundation operates under the overarching principle of Governance Before Collapse: the conviction that governance systems must be designed to detect, deliberate, and act upon early-warning signals before thresholds of irreversibility are crossed. This principle informs every provision of this Manual and every decision made under its authority.

Clause 1.4 — Controlling Language

The official and controlling version of this Manual is drafted in English, which is the Foundation's governing language for corporate records, Board governance, and U.S. regulatory compliance. Portuguese translations may be prepared for educational or collaborative purposes but shall not constitute the controlling version for any legal, fiduciary, or regulatory matter.

02

Governance Principles

Clause 2.1 — The Three Fiduciary Duties

Every Director, Officer, and person exercising delegated authority on behalf of the Foundation shall discharge their duties in accordance with the following fiduciary duties, which are non-delegable and enforceable under Missouri law and IRS oversight:

Duty of Care

Act with the care an ordinarily prudent person in a like position would exercise under similar circumstances. This includes attending meetings, reviewing materials in advance, asking informed questions, and seeking independent advice when necessary.

Duty of Loyalty

Act in good faith in a manner the Director reasonably believes to be in the best interests of the Foundation. This requires placing the Foundation's interests above personal, familial, or business interests and avoiding self-dealing.

Duty of Obedience

Act in furtherance of the Foundation's purpose and mission as stated in its Articles of Incorporation and tax-exemption application. This includes ensuring that organizational activities remain within the scope of 501(c)(3) charitable purposes and that assets are used exclusively for exempt purposes.

Clause 2.2 — Governance Before Collapse

The Foundation's governance architecture is designed to operate before systems fail. This means:

  • Board deliberation prioritizes early detection over reactive response;
  • Scientific calibration (under the c-ECO Governance Framework) informs strategic thresholds;
  • Financial and operational reserves are maintained to absorb shocks without compromising mission;
  • Conflict-of-interest and ethics frameworks are preventive, not merely punitive;
  • Succession planning and institutional memory are treated as governance infrastructure, not afterthoughts.

Clause 2.3 — Separation of Powers

The Foundation maintains a clear separation between:

  • Board Governance (fiduciary oversight, strategic direction, policy approval);
  • Executive Management (day-to-day operations, program execution, resource deployment);
  • Scientific Governance (methodological integrity, calibration, threshold validation — exercised by the Calibration Council under the c-ECO Governance Framework, independent from operational management).

No single individual shall simultaneously hold the offices of President and Treasurer. The offices of President and Treasurer shall remain separate to preserve sound fiduciary governance and internal controls.

Clause 2.4 — Transparency and Accountability

The Foundation shall operate with transparency appropriate to its stage and capacity, including:

  • Publication of this Governance Manual (in unredacted form, excluding confidential personnel or proprietary scientific matters) on the Foundation's website;
  • Annual filing of IRS Form 990-PF and Missouri charitable registration;
  • Annual reports to donors, partners, and the public summarizing programmatic and financial performance;
  • Contemporaneous documentation of all Board and committee meetings.

Board Responsibilities

Clause 3.1 — Size and Composition

The Board shall consist of seven (7) voting Directors. This size is selected to balance diverse perspectives with efficient decision-making. The Board shall be composed of persons committed to the Foundation's mission, with a mix of competencies including scientific expertise, financial literacy, legal acumen, fundraising capacity, and Amazonian or ecological domain knowledge.

The Board shall include at least three (3) Independent Directors, as defined by IRS tests and applicable Missouri law. Independent Directors shall have no material financial, familial, or business relationships that could impair objective judgment.

Clause 3.2 — Election and Terms

Directors shall be elected by the Board for staggered terms of three (3) years, with no more than one-third (1/3) of the Board rotating in any given year. Directors may serve up to two (2) consecutive terms (six years total), after which they must rotate off for at least one (1) year before re-election. The Board may waive the term limit for a specific Director by two-thirds (2/3) supermajority vote, documented in the minutes with rationale.

Clause 3.3 — Removal

A Director may be removed by two-thirds (2/3) vote of the entire Board for Cause. "Cause" includes: (a) breach of fiduciary duty; (b) material violation of this Manual, the Bylaws, or the Code of Ethics; (c) prolonged absence from Board meetings (more than two consecutive unexcused absences); (d) conviction of a felony or crime involving moral turpitude; or (e) conduct that brings material reputational harm to the Foundation. The Director in question shall be given written notice and an opportunity to respond before the removal vote.

Clause 3.4 — Board Leadership

The Board shall elect a President (who may also hold the title Chair of the Board) to serve as the principal leader of the Board. The President shall:

  • Organize the Board agenda with input from the President of the Foundation and management;
  • Lead Board discussions of executive performance and compensation in executive sessions without management present;
  • Encourage frank but collegial discussions at the Board level and between the Board and management;
  • Represent the Board in external communications, unless otherwise delegated.

No employee of the Foundation may serve as President or hold any other Board title with similar responsibilities, unless approved by a two-thirds (2/3) majority of the entire Board, documented in writing with rationale. At this stage, the President of the Foundation (Jacqueline Alves) is a Director and serves as President of the Board; this dual role is disclosed and shall be reviewed annually for continued appropriateness.

Clause 3.5 — Onboarding and Continuing Education

Each new Director shall complete a structured orientation within ninety (90) days of election, covering:

  • The Foundation's mission, history, programs, and theory of change;
  • This Manual, the Bylaws, and the Articles of Incorporation;
  • Fiduciary duties under Missouri law and IRS requirements;
  • Financial statements, budget, and key risks;
  • The c-ECO Governance Framework, TFP, and TDR at a governance level (not technical depth);
  • Conflict of interest disclosure and ethics expectations.

The Board shall dedicate at least one meeting per year to continuing education on topics relevant to the Foundation's mission and governance obligations.

Officers

The Officers of the Foundation, as required by Missouri law and this Manual, shall be:

Vice PresidentYesPrepared to assume leadership if President is unavailable
TreasurerYesMust be separate from President; responsible for fiduciary oversight of finances
SecretaryYesResponsible for minutes, records, and corporate filings

Additional officers (e.g., Assistant Secretary, Assistant Treasurer) may be appointed by the Board as needed.

Clause 4.2 — President

The President shall be the chief executive officer of the Foundation, responsible for:

  • Setting the strategic and scientific direction of the Foundation, in alignment with Board-approved plans;
  • Overseeing all programs, operations, and personnel;
  • Representing the Foundation in external relations, partnerships, and public communications;
  • Preparing and presenting the Annual Operational Plan and budget to the Board;
  • Ensuring compliance with all applicable laws, regulations, and policies;
  • Appointing and supervising Executive Officers, subject to Board confirmation for senior roles;
  • Serving as the primary liaison between the Board and management.

The President may also hold the title of Scientific Director in the executive organogram, reflecting their scientific leadership role. For governance document purposes, the statutory title is President.

Clause 4.3 — Treasurer

The Treasurer shall have primary responsibility for the oversight of the Foundation's finances, including:

  • Overseeing the preparation and accuracy of financial statements;
  • Monitoring cash flow, reserves, and investment performance;
  • Presenting financial reports to the Board and the Audit & Finance Committee;
  • Ensuring compliance with IRS, Missouri, and donor-imposed financial requirements;
  • Co-signing or authorizing financial transactions above delegated thresholds;
  • Serving as the principal liaison with the independent auditor (if any).

The Treasurer need not be a professional accountant but must be financially literate. The Board may engage an outside bookkeeper, accounting firm, or Chief Financial Officer to assist the Treasurer.

Clause 4.4 — Secretary

The Secretary shall be responsible for:

  • Recording, maintaining, and distributing minutes of all Board and committee meetings;
  • Maintaining the Foundation's corporate records, including this Manual, Bylaws, Articles, and policies;
  • Ensuring timely filing of annual reports, charitable registrations, and other state and federal filings;
  • Maintaining the roster of Directors, Officers, and key persons;
  • Administering the document control system (Appendix B).

Clause 4.5 — Terms and Succession

Officers shall serve one-year terms, renewable annually by Board vote. The Governance & Nominations Committee shall maintain a succession plan for each officer position, identifying potential successors 12–18 months before terms expire.

Executive Leadership

Clause 5.1 — Founder-Led Structure

The Foundation is currently founder-led. Jacqueline Alves serves as President and, in the executive organogram, as Scientific Director, supported by Executive Officers appointed by the Board. There is no separate CEO or Executive Director at this stage. The Board retains fiduciary oversight while day-to-day management is delegated to the President and Executive Officers.

Note: The founder-led structure is appropriate for the Foundation's current stage but shall be reviewed at each Strategic Planning cycle. The Board shall develop a transition plan to a non-founder-led executive structure when the Foundation's scale, funding, or governance maturity warrants it.

Clause 5.2 — Executive Officers

The President may appoint Executive Officers to manage specific functions, subject to Board confirmation for roles with material budgetary or personnel authority. Executive Officers may include:

  • Chief Operating Officer (or equivalent);
  • Chief Financial Officer (or equivalent);
  • Program Directors (Fellowship, Living Labs, Observatory, Research & Publications, Executive Education, Policy & Institutional Transformation);
  • Head of Development and Partnerships;
  • Head of Communications and Public Affairs.

Executive Officers serve at the pleasure of the President, subject to any contractual terms. Their performance shall be reviewed annually by the President, with input from the Compensation Committee.

Clause 5.3 — Delegation of Authority

The Board delegates day-to-day operational authority to the President, who may further delegate to Executive Officers. The scope of delegation shall be documented in the Delegation of Authority Policy and Signature Authority Policy, which specify:

  • Monetary thresholds for individual, dual, and Board approval;
  • Categories of transactions requiring Board or committee approval;
  • Signing authority for contracts, grants, and financial instruments;
  • Emergency authority and its limits.

Delegation does not relieve the Board of fiduciary oversight. The Board shall review delegation policies annually.

Clause 6.3 — Special Committees

The Board may establish special (ad hoc) committees for specific purposes (e.g., strategic planning, merger evaluation, crisis response). Special committees shall have a defined mandate, timeline, and sunset clause. They shall not exercise delegated decision-making authority unless expressly authorized by Board resolution.

Clause 6.4 — Limitations

No committee shall have authority to: (a) amend the Articles of Incorporation or Bylaws; (b) elect or remove Directors; (c) approve a merger, dissolution, or sale of substantially all assets; (d) approve the Foundation's annual budget or Strategic Plan; or (e) take any action reserved to the full Board by Missouri law or this Manual.

Decision-Making

Clause 8.1 — Standard of Decision-Making

All Board decisions shall be made with the care, loyalty, and obedience required by Clause 2.1. Directors shall come to each decision informed by relevant materials, independent advice when necessary, and collegial deliberation. Decisions shall be documented in the minutes, including the basis for the decision and any dissenting views.

Clause 8.2 — Levels of Authority

The Foundation recognizes three levels of decision-making authority:

Board-Reserved Decisions

Strategic direction, budget approval, executive compensation, major transactions, governance amendments, and matters reserved by law. Require Board vote.

Committee-Delegated Decisions

Matters within a committee's charter (e.g., audit scope, compensation levels within policy, governance policy drafts). Require committee vote; reported to Board.

Management-Delegated Decisions

Operational matters within approved budgets and policies. Delegated to the President and Executive Officers; reported to Board through regular updates.

Clause 8.3 — Emergency Decisions

In emergencies where Board action is required but a quorum cannot be assembled, the President may take necessary action to protect the Foundation's assets, reputation, or mission, provided that:

  • The emergency is documented in writing within 48 hours;
  • The action is reported to the Board at its next meeting (or by special meeting if warranted);
  • The action does not exceed $25,000 in financial commitment without subsequent Board ratification;
  • The action is consistent with the Foundation's mission and existing policies.
09

Meetings

Clause 9.1 — Regular Meetings

The Board shall hold at least four (4) regular meetings per fiscal year, scheduled in advance via an annualized calendar. Regular meetings shall address strategic direction, financial oversight, program review, and governance matters. Meeting dates shall be set at least six (6) months in advance.

Clause 9.2 — Special Meetings

Special meetings may be called by the President, the President of the Board, or any two (2) Directors. Notice of a special meeting shall be given at least forty-eight (48) hours in advance, specifying the purpose. No business other than the stated purpose shall be transacted at a special meeting unless all Directors present consent.

Clause 9.3 — Remote Participation

Directors may participate in meetings by video conference, telephone, or other real-time electronic means, provided that all participants can hear and be heard. Remote participation counts toward quorum and voting. The Secretary shall document the remote attendance method in the minutes.

Clause 9.4 — Executive Sessions

The Board shall conduct executive sessions (meetings without management present) at least twice per year, and as needed for sensitive matters. Executive sessions may be held by the full Board or by individual committees. Minutes of executive sessions shall be kept confidential and distributed only to Directors, unless the Board determines otherwise.

Clause 9.5 — Minutes

Minutes shall be contemporaneously documented for all Board and committee meetings. Minutes shall include:

  • Date, time, location, and attendance (including remote participants);
  • Matters discussed, with sufficient detail to demonstrate fiduciary deliberation;
  • Decisions made, including vote tallies and recusals;
  • Action items with responsible parties and deadlines.

Draft minutes shall be circulated within fourteen (14) days of the meeting and approved at the next meeting. Minutes shall be maintained permanently in the corporate records.

10

Voting

Clause 10.1 — Quorum

A quorum for Board meetings shall consist of a majority of the Directors then in office. For a seven-member Board, a quorum is four (4) Directors. No action may be taken without a quorum, except to adjourn to a later time. A Director who recuses themselves from a matter shall still count toward quorum for that meeting.

Clause 10.2 — Majority Vote

Routine Board actions require approval by a majority of the Directors present at a meeting with quorum. A majority means more than 50% of the votes cast by Directors entitled to vote on the matter.

Clause 10.3 — Supermajority Vote

The following actions require approval by a two-thirds (2/3) supermajority of the Directors then in office:

  • Amendment of the Articles of Incorporation or Bylaws;
  • Dissolution, merger, or sale of substantially all assets;
  • Removal of a Director or Officer for Cause;
  • Approval of a related-party transaction above a material threshold;
  • Adoption or material amendment of this Governance Manual;
  • Approval of the five-year Strategic Plan;
  • Any other matter designated as "fundamental" by the Board.

Clause 10.4 — Recusal

A Director with a conflict of interest (actual or apparent) on a matter before the Board shall disclose the conflict, abstain from deliberation and voting, and leave the meeting during discussion of the matter, unless invited to provide background information before deliberations begin. The recusal shall be documented in the minutes.

Clause 10.5 — Financial Delegation Thresholds

Specific monetary thresholds for financial approvals (e.g., contracts, grants, expenditures) shall be established in the separate Delegation of Authority Policy and Signature Authority Policy, not in this Manual. Those policies shall be reviewed annually by the Audit & Finance Committee and approved by the Board.

Ethics

Clause 11.1 — Code of Business Conduct and Ethics

The Foundation adopts a Code of Business Conduct and Ethics applicable to all Directors, Officers, Executive Officers, employees, volunteers, and advisors. The Code shall require fulfillment of responsibilities in a manner that furthers the Foundation's purpose and mission and complies with law, regulations, ethical standards, and policies adopted by the Foundation.

Clause 11.2 — Whistleblower Policy

The Foundation shall maintain a Whistleblower Policy containing procedures to receive, investigate, and take appropriate action regarding fraud or non-compliance with law or organizational policy, and to protect whistleblowers against retaliation. The policy shall:

  • Encourage officers, Directors, employees, and volunteers to come forward with credible information regarding illegal practices or violations of organizational policies, law, regulations, and/or ethical standards;
  • Specify that the organization will protect the individual from retaliation;
  • Identify those staff, Board members, or outside parties to whom such information can be reported, including an anonymous reporting mechanism;
  • Require investigation within a reasonable timeframe and documentation of findings.

Clause 11.3 — Document Retention and Destruction

The Foundation shall maintain a Document Retention and Destruction Policy promoting the responsible stewardship of information by ensuring that documents are retained for appropriate periods, sensitive information is protected, and unnecessary records are disposed of in a consistent manner. The policy shall identify record retention responsibilities of staff, volunteers, and Board members and comply with applicable provisions of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act regarding obstruction of justice.

Clause 11.4 — AI Governance

The Foundation may use artificial intelligence tools for internal productivity, communications, development, and research. AI use shall be governed by internal guidelines specifying:

  • Permitted use cases and prohibited applications;
  • Data input restrictions and confidentiality protections;
  • Requirements for human oversight of AI-generated work;
  • Procedures for evaluating and approving new AI tools;
  • Accountability for errors or misinformation produced by AI systems.

Conflict of Interest

Clause 12.1 — Definition

A conflict of interest arises when a Director, Officer, or other decision-maker has an outside interest or relationship that conflicts or may conflict with their ability to act strictly in the best interests of the Foundation. Conflicts may be financial, familial, relational, or reputational.

A conflict is actual when the outside interest directly interferes with fiduciary duty. It is apparent when a reasonable observer could perceive that the outside interest might interfere, even if the fiduciary believes otherwise. Both actual and apparent conflicts shall be disclosed and managed.

Clause 12.2 — Annual Disclosure

All Directors, Officers, key persons, and advisors shall complete an annual conflict-of-interest questionnaire disclosing:

  • All business entities in which the person or a family member has a substantial financial interest (generally defined as ownership of 5% or more, or a board or executive role);
  • All transactions or proposed transactions between the Foundation and such entities, regardless of amount;
  • All compensation, loans, or benefits received from the Foundation or related organizations in the prior fiscal year;
  • Any other relationship — including advisory roles, consultancies, or family employment — that could impair independence or objectivity.

Disclosures shall be reviewed by the Audit & Finance Committee (or the full Board if no Audit & Finance Committee exists) and maintained in secure corporate records for the duration of service plus seven (7) years.

Clause 12.3 — Transaction Review Procedures

When a potential or actual conflict is identified, the following procedure shall apply:

  1. Disclosure. The interested person shall disclose the conflict to the Board or relevant committee before deliberation begins, verbally and in writing.
  2. Recusal. The interested person shall leave the meeting during deliberation and voting, unless invited to answer questions before deliberations commence. They may not vote or lobby.
  3. Independent Review. The remaining Directors shall determine whether a conflict exists and whether the transaction is fair, reasonable, and in the Foundation's best interests.
  4. Comparability Data. For transactions above a material threshold (to be defined in the Conflict of Interest Policy), the Board shall obtain comparability data and document the basis for approval.
  5. Documentation. The decision, rationale, and recusal shall be documented in the minutes. The minutes shall state that the transaction was approved by disinterested Directors.

Clause 12.4 — Prohibited Transactions

The following are prohibited without prior Board approval and full disclosure:

  • Loans to Directors, Officers, or their family members, directly or indirectly;
  • Excess benefit transactions as defined by IRS Section 4958 (intermediate sanctions);
  • Business transactions with entities in which a Director or Officer has a substantial interest, unless at arm's-length terms demonstrably favorable to the Foundation;
  • Use of Foundation assets, personnel, or reputation for personal benefit or private inurement;
  • Compensation arrangements that are not set using the IRS rebuttable presumption procedure.
IRS Safe Harbor: The Foundation shall follow the IRS rebuttable presumption procedure for setting compensation of disqualified persons, including: (a) comparability data; (b) approval by an independent body (Compensation Committee or full Board without the interested person present); and (c) contemporaneous documentation of the deliberation and basis for approval.

Clause 12.5 — Related-Party Transactions

A related-party transaction is any transaction in which a Director, Officer, key person, or their family member has a direct or indirect financial interest. All related-party transactions, regardless of amount, shall be:

  • Disclosed in advance to the Audit & Finance Committee;
  • Reviewed for fairness and mission alignment;
  • Approved only by disinterested Directors;
  • Reported on Schedule L of IRS Form 990-PF when required.

Annual Planning

Clause 13.1 — Annual Operational Planning Cycle

The Foundation shall maintain an Annual Operational Planning (AOP) cycle with a one-year horizon, aligned with the fiscal year (July 1 – June 30). The AOP translates the five-year Strategic Plan into concrete objectives, budgets, and milestones for the upcoming year.

The AOP cycle shall follow this calendar:

MonthActivityResponsible
MarchProgram Directors submit draft operational plans and budget requestsPresident / Executive Officers
AprilAudit & Finance Committee reviews consolidated budget; Calibration Council reviews scientific milestonesAudit & Finance Committee / Calibration Council
MayBoard reviews and approves annual budget and operational planFull Board
JuneFinal adjustments; communication of approved plan to all staff and partnersPresident
JulyFiscal year begins; quarterly review schedule activatedAll

Clause 13.2 — Components of the Annual Plan

Each Annual Operational Plan shall include, at minimum:

  • Program Objectives: Specific, measurable outcomes for each institutional program, including the Fellowship Program, Living Labs, Observatory Program, Research & Publications Program, Executive Education & Training, and Policy & Institutional Transformation, aligned with the Foundation's Strategic Plan;
  • Financial Budget: Line-item budget for revenue and expenses, including restricted and unrestricted funds, with variance thresholds;
  • Fundraising Targets: Goals for grants, individual donations, institutional partnerships, and earned revenue;
  • Scientific Milestones: Threshold calibrations, publication targets, data collection schedules, and Living Lab deployment plans;
  • Human Resources Plan: Headcount, hiring timeline, professional development, and succession readiness;
  • Risk Register: Updated assessment of operational, financial, reputational, and scientific risks with mitigation strategies;
  • Compliance Calendar: Deadlines for IRS filings, state registrations, audit schedules, and policy reviews.

Clause 13.3 — Quarterly Review and Variance Reporting

The Board shall receive quarterly variance reports comparing actual performance against the Annual Plan. Reports shall include:

  • Financial performance (revenue vs. budget, expense vs. budget, reserve levels);
  • Programmatic progress (milestones achieved, delays, and corrective actions);
  • Fundraising progress (pipeline, closed gifts, pending applications);
  • Risk updates (new risks, escalated risks, mitigated risks);
  • Compliance status (upcoming deadlines, completed filings, policy reviews).

Material variances (generally defined as >10% of budget line or >$5,000, whichever is lower) shall trigger a written explanation and, if necessary, a Board vote on a revised plan or budget transfer.

Clause 13.4 — Mid-Year Adjustment

The Board may approve a mid-year adjustment to the Annual Plan if material changes in funding, programmatic context, or risk environment warrant it. Mid-year adjustments require the same review and approval process as the original Annual Plan. The President shall present a written justification, including impact on the Strategic Plan, budget reallocation, and risk implications.

Strategic Planning

Clause 14.1 — Five-Year Strategic Plan

The Foundation shall maintain a five-year Strategic Plan that articulates the Foundation's long-term vision, theory of change, programmatic priorities, and resource strategy. The Strategic Plan is the Board's principal instrument for setting direction and evaluating executive performance. It shall be reviewed annually and comprehensively revised every five (5) years.

Clause 14.2 — Strategic Plan Contents

The Strategic Plan shall include, at minimum:

  • Mission & Vision Statement: A clear, compelling articulation of the Foundation's purpose and aspirational future state, grounded in the Governance Before Collapse principle;
  • Theory of Change: The causal logic linking the Foundation's activities to desired outcomes, including assumptions, risks, and external factors;
  • Programmatic Pillars: The core program areas (Fellowship, Living Labs, Observatory, Research & Publications, Executive Education, Policy & Institutional Transformation) with objectives, indicators, and milestones for the planning period;
  • Financial Strategy: Revenue diversification targets, reserve policy, investment strategy, and capital expenditure plan;
  • Organizational Development: Governance maturity targets, staffing plan, infrastructure needs, and succession roadmap;
  • Partnership & Ecosystem Strategy: Key institutional relationships, geographic focus, and collaborative frameworks (including the c-ECO network and Amazon Living Lab);
  • Risk & Resilience Framework: Identification of strategic risks (financial, operational, reputational, scientific, geopolitical) and resilience strategies, including the c-ECO macro-prudential approach;
  • Scientific Calibration Agenda: Priority thresholds for TFP/TDR calibration, publication roadmap, and data infrastructure investments.

Clause 14.3 — Strategic Planning Process

The Strategic Plan shall be developed through a participatory process involving:

  • Board Retreat: A dedicated Board retreat (minimum one full day) held in Year 5 of the current plan to initiate the next planning cycle;
  • Stakeholder Input: Structured consultation with Program Directors, the Calibration Council, Scientific Advisory Board, key donors, and partner institutions;
  • Environmental Scan: Analysis of trends in ecological economics, Amazonian governance, climate finance, technology, and regulatory landscapes;
  • Gap Analysis: Assessment of the Foundation's current capabilities against the aspirations of the new plan;
  • Draft & Review: Draft prepared by the President, reviewed by the Governance & Nominations Committee, and presented to the Board for debate and amendment;
  • Board Adoption: Final approval by two-thirds (2/3) supermajority vote, as required by Clause 10.3.

Clause 14.4 — Annual Strategic Review

Each year, the Board shall conduct a strategic review assessing:

  • Progress toward five-year objectives, with quantitative indicators where possible;
  • Changes in the external environment that may require plan adaptation;
  • Lessons learned from program evaluation, audits, and stakeholder feedback;
  • Alignment between the Annual Operational Plan and the Strategic Plan;
  • Governance maturity and organizational capacity gaps.

The annual strategic review shall be documented in a written report, presented at the Board meeting following the fiscal year-end, and published (in summary form) on the Foundation's website.

Clause 14.5 — Transition Planning

The Strategic Plan shall include a founder transition roadmap that addresses the evolution from founder-led governance to institutionalized leadership. The roadmap shall identify:

  • Triggers for transition (e.g., funding milestones, Board maturity, organizational scale);
  • Competencies required in a non-founder President/CEO;
  • Timeline and phasing for delegation, recruitment, and handover;
  • Role of the Founder in post-transition governance (e.g., Scientific Director Emeritus, Board member, or senior advisor);
  • Communication strategy for staff, donors, and partners.
Note: The founder transition is not a sign of failure but of institutional maturity. The Board shall treat this as a strategic priority, not an administrative afterthought.

Performance Review

Clause 15.1 — Board Self-Assessment

The Board shall conduct an annual self-assessment evaluating its effectiveness in fulfilling fiduciary duties, governing strategically, and supporting the Foundation's mission. The assessment shall cover:

  • Attendance, preparation, and engagement at meetings;
  • Quality of deliberation, debate, and decision-making;
  • Composition, diversity, and skill gaps;
  • Relationship with management and advisory bodies;
  • Performance of committees and individual Directors;
  • Adherence to this Manual and governance best practices.

The Governance & Nominations Committee shall administer the self-assessment, compile results, and present findings with recommendations for improvement to the Board. Results shall be kept confidential to the Board.

Clause 15.2 — President Performance Evaluation

The President shall be evaluated annually by the full Board (in executive session, without the President present), led by the Compensation Committee or the President of the Board. The evaluation shall assess:

  • Achievement of Annual Plan and Strategic Plan objectives;
  • Financial stewardship and budget management;
  • Programmatic impact and scientific quality;
  • External relations, partnership development, and fundraising;
  • Leadership of staff and organizational culture;
  • Compliance, risk management, and ethical conduct;
  • Board relations and communication.

The evaluation shall be documented in writing, shared with the President, and used to set performance goals and compensation for the following year. A 360-degree component (input from Executive Officers and key staff) may be included at the Board's discretion.

Clause 15.3 — Executive Officer Performance Review

The President shall conduct annual performance reviews of all Executive Officers, using criteria aligned with their role descriptions and the Annual Plan. Reviews shall include:

  • Objective achievement against agreed Key Performance Indicators (KPIs);
  • Competency assessment (leadership, technical, collaborative);
  • Professional development needs and opportunities;
  • Compensation adjustment recommendations, subject to Compensation Committee approval.

Executive Officer performance reviews shall be documented and maintained in confidential personnel records.

Clause 15.4 — Program Evaluation

Each institutional program shall undergo an annual evaluation assessing:

  • Relevance to the Foundation's mission and Strategic Plan;
  • Effectiveness in achieving stated outcomes;
  • Efficiency in resource use;
  • Quality of scientific output, methodological rigor, and peer engagement;
  • Stakeholder satisfaction (fellows, partners, communities, donors);
  • Sustainability and scalability potential.

Program evaluations shall be conducted by the relevant Program Director with input from the Calibration Council and Scientific Advisory Board. Findings shall be reported to the Board and used to inform the next Annual Plan.

Clause 15.5 — External Evaluation

At least once every five (5) years, the Board shall commission an independent external evaluation of the Foundation's governance, programs, and organizational health. The external evaluator shall be selected by the Governance & Nominations Committee and shall report directly to the Board. The evaluation shall assess:

  • Organizational governance maturity against sector benchmarks;
  • Program impact and theory of change validity;
  • Financial health and sustainability;
  • Compliance with law, regulation, and donor requirements;
  • Recommendations for improvement.

The Board shall respond to the external evaluation with a written action plan, approved within ninety (90) days of receiving the report.

16

Continuous Improvement

Clause 16.1 — Governance Maturity Model

The Foundation adopts a Governance Maturity Model to guide its evolution from early-stage to fully institutionalized governance. The model recognizes five stages:

StageCharacteristicsTarget Timeline
1. FoundationalFounder-led, informal processes, small Board, limited documentationYears 1–2
2. EmergingBasic policies in place, Board committees formed, initial strategic planningYears 2–4
3. DevelopingFull policy suite, annual planning cycle, performance reviews, external auditYears 4–6
4. MatureIndependent governance, diversified funding, robust evaluation, sector leadershipYears 6–10
5. ExemplaryThought leadership, governance innovation, replication of models, global recognitionYears 10+

The Board shall assess the Foundation's maturity stage annually and identify specific actions to advance to the next stage. The Governance & Nominations Committee shall maintain a maturity action plan.

Clause 16.2 — Innovation and Learning

The Foundation shall cultivate a culture of adaptive learning and governance innovation. This includes:

  • Regular exposure to emerging practices in nonprofit governance, ecological economics, and systems science;
  • Piloting new governance tools (e.g., pre-mortem analysis, red-teaming, scenario planning) in Board deliberations;
  • Documenting lessons learned from successes and failures, and integrating them into policy and practice;
  • Encouraging constructive dissent and intellectual honesty in Board and committee discussions;
  • Investing in professional development for Directors, Officers, and staff.

Clause 16.3 — Manual Amendment

This Manual may be amended by a two-thirds (2/3) supermajority vote of the entire Board, as required by Clause 10.3. Proposed amendments shall be:

  • Drafted by the Governance & Nominations Committee or the President, with legal review if material;
  • Circulated to all Directors at least fourteen (14) days before the vote;
  • Accompanied by a written rationale explaining the need for the amendment and its implications;
  • Documented in the minutes, with the amended text archived per Appendix B.

Amendments shall be reflected in the version control system (Appendix B) and communicated to all Directors, Officers, and key personnel within seven (7) days of adoption.

Clause 16.4 — Institutional Memory

The Foundation shall maintain robust institutional memory to ensure continuity across leadership transitions. This includes:

  • Comprehensive, well-organized corporate records (minutes, contracts, policies, evaluations);
  • A documented history of strategic decisions, including rationale and lessons learned;
  • Knowledge management systems for scientific methodologies, partnerships, and stakeholder relationships;
  • Oral history interviews with founders and key leaders, archived for future reference;
  • Board alumni network to retain access to historical perspective and expertise.

The Secretary, in coordination with the President, shall be responsible for institutional memory systems.

Clause 16.5 — Legacy and Impact

The Foundation's ultimate measure of success is not its perpetuity but its impact. The Board shall periodically consider:

  • Whether the Foundation's mission might be better served through merger, partnership, or spin-off;
  • Whether the Foundation's assets should be spent down (sunset) or preserved in perpetuity (endowment);
  • How the Foundation's models, tools, and frameworks (TFP, TDR, SIL, ESL) can be disseminated and replicated;
  • What legacy the Foundation seeks to leave in Amazonian governance and ecological economics.
Principle: The Foundation exists to serve its mission, not itself. Governance must remain humble, adaptive, and oriented toward impact rather than institutional preservation.

Glossary

Defined Terms

Annual Operational Plan (AOP)
The Foundation's one-year operational and financial plan, translating the Strategic Plan into concrete objectives, budgets, and milestones for a single fiscal year.
Articles of Incorporation
The foundational corporate document filed with the State of Missouri establishing the Foundation as a nonprofit corporation. Controls over this Manual and the Bylaws in case of conflict.
Board
The Board of Directors of the Johann Christian Hasse Foundation, exercising fiduciary oversight and strategic governance.
Bylaws
The corporate bylaws of the Foundation, governing internal procedures such as meetings, voting, and officer duties. Subordinate to the Articles of Incorporation but controlling over this Manual.
Calibration Council
The scientific governance body under the c-ECO Governance Framework responsible for methodological integrity, threshold validation, and peer review of TFP/TDR calibrations.
Cause (for removal)
Grounds for removal of a Director or Officer, including breach of fiduciary duty, material policy violation, prolonged unexcused absence, felony conviction, or conduct causing reputational harm.
c-ECO Governance Framework
The systemic governance methodology developed by c-ECO (ecological economics and sustainability) that integrates scientific calibration, threshold dynamics, and anticipatory governance into organizational decision-making.
Conflict of Interest
A situation in which a Director, Officer, or decision-maker has an outside interest or relationship that conflicts or may conflict with their ability to act in the Foundation's best interests.
Director
A member of the Board of Directors with voting authority and fiduciary duties.
Duty of Care
The fiduciary obligation to act with the care an ordinarily prudent person in a like position would exercise under similar circumstances.
Duty of Loyalty
The fiduciary obligation to act in good faith in the best interests of the Foundation, placing organizational interests above personal interests.
Duty of Obedience
The fiduciary obligation to ensure that the Foundation's activities further its stated mission and comply with applicable laws and regulations.
ESL
Economic Sustainability Laboratory — a c-ECO program and framework for applied research in ecological economics and sustainability governance.
Executive Officer
A senior staff member appointed by the President to manage a specific function (e.g., COO, CFO, Program Director), subject to Board confirmation for roles with material authority.
Founder-Led
An organizational stage in which the founder (Jacqueline Alves) serves as both President and primary strategic leader, with governance evolving toward institutionalized leadership.
Governance Before Collapse
The Foundation's overarching principle: governance systems must detect, deliberate, and act upon early-warning signals before thresholds of irreversibility are crossed.
Independent Director
A Director who has no material financial, familial, or business relationship that could impair objective judgment, as determined by IRS tests and Missouri law.
IRS Form 990-PF
The annual information return filed by private foundations with the U.S. Internal Revenue Service, disclosing finances, activities, governance, and compliance.
Key Person
An individual (Director, Officer, or highly compensated employee) whose compensation, transactions, or relationships must be disclosed on IRS Form 990-PF.
Living Lab
A c-ECO applied research and engagement platform (e.g., the Amazon Living Lab) integrating scientific observation, community participation, and policy experimentation in a real-world setting.
Officer
A statutory corporate officer (President, Vice President, Treasurer, Secretary) elected by the Board, with duties defined by Missouri law and this Manual.
Quorum
The minimum number of Directors required to be present for a Board meeting to transact business. For the Foundation, a majority of Directors in office (four of seven).
Related-Party Transaction
Any transaction in which a Director, Officer, key person, or their family member has a direct or indirect financial interest.
SIL
Systemic Intelligence Library — a c-ECO knowledge architecture and analytical framework for sectoral intelligence and systemic risk assessment.
Strategic Plan
The Foundation's five-year directional document articulating vision, priorities, resource strategy, and impact theory.
Supermajority
A vote requiring approval by two-thirds (2/3) of the Directors then in office, reserved for fundamental governance decisions per Clause 10.3.
TDR
Threshold Dynamics Research — the c-ECO scientific methodology for identifying, calibrating, and monitoring systemic thresholds and tipping points.
TFP
Theoretical Framework for Practice — the c-ECO conceptual architecture linking ecological economics, governance science, and applied research to institutional action.
Whistleblower
An individual who reports fraud, illegal practices, or policy violations through the Foundation's Whistleblower Policy, protected against retaliation.
