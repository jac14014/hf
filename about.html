Foundation Origin

From the Amazon
to Systemic Governance

The Johann Christian Hasse Foundation was not born in a library. It was born from 22 years of running businesses in Belém do Pará — where contracts failed, supply chains collapsed, and systemic risk was not a concept but a daily reality.

The Story Behind the Framework

1993 — 2015 — For twenty-two years, Jacqueline A. Ennis built and managed hospitality, tourism, and cultural enterprises in Belém do Pará, including operations conducted through Amazon Rainforest Tour Ltda. Her work brought together regional cultural initiatives, artists, Indigenous and riverine communities, tourism operators, public institutions, suppliers, and visitors from Brazil and abroad. Operating in one of the world's most complex socio-environmental regions, she oversaw contracts, procurement, logistics, events, and supply chains while navigating continuous ecological, economic, legal, and institutional uncertainty. These experiences provided firsthand insight into how governance, markets, and contractual systems become increasingly fragile as underlying environmental and social conditions change—forming the empirical foundation for her later research on Threshold Dynamics and the development of pre-threshold governance frameworks.

2016 — 2021 — From Brazil to Boston. Paralegal Studies at Boston University, working as Contract Administrator at Bluebird Network, observing the same patterns: contracts designed for stability that become traps when external conditions change.

2021 — 2023 — LL.M. in Business Law at Washington University in St. Louis. Formalization of the Recognition-Action Gap: legal systems recognize harm only after it materializes. There is no juridical category for pre-threshold responsibility.

2023 — 2024 — COP30 and the Birth of the Doctrine. When Belém was selected to host COP30, the c-ECO framework crystallized as a legal architecture for anticipatory governance.

Mission, Vision, and Programs

Mission

The Foundation operates on a strictly non-profit basis to benefit the community by advancing education and improving social welfare. We provide free open-source frameworks, public educational resources, and civic mentorship to protect public infrastructure and build community resilience against environmental and systemic risks.

Vision

A global civic education and public-benefit research ecosystem where communities, public-welfare managers, and local resilience leaders can access practical tools, open frameworks, and mentorship before environmental and systemic risks become humanitarian crises.

c-ECO Fellowship Program

The c-ECO Fellowship Program is the flagship humanitarian and community education initiative of the Johann Christian Hasse Foundation. It provides open-access training and collaborative mentorship in civic governance, systemic risk mitigation, and local community resilience through applied public-interest projects, civic partnerships, and free open-source frameworks. The program prepares grassroots community leaders, disaster-response coordinators, and public-welfare managers to implement resilience principles to address complex environmental and public infrastructure challenges, directly protecting vulnerable populations from systemic harm.

c-ECO: Predictive Governance & Systemic Resilience Initiative

The c-ECO Initiative develops open-source risk-mitigation methodologies, provides free public educational resources, and supports collaborative civic projects to improve community decision-making under uncertainty. The program focuses strictly on the early detection of public risks and the implementation of practical resilience tools across environmental, public infrastructure, and civil society systems to prevent humanitarian crises and protect public welfare.

Institutional Lineage

The Foundation traces its juridical lineage to Johann Christian Hasse (1779–1830), a German jurist whose work on institutional continuity and legal memory informs the Foundation's archival mission. The name carries a commitment to scholarship that outlives its authors.

Established in February 2026, the Foundation operates as an institution of record: quiet, researched, legally literate, and built for longevity. We preserve institutional memory as a public asset, develop legal frameworks for systemic and ecological risk, and translate archival continuity into research, doctrine, and field action.
